# Source Analysis

The reframe library and tonality patterns in this skill were
reverse-engineered from a corpus of 50 Instagram Reel transcripts of
a high-ticket sales operator (Andres Contreras / The Impact Team).
This file documents which patterns came from which transcripts, so
each move can be traced back to its source and verified.

---

## Why this corpus

A few features made it unusually clean to derive a framework from:

1. **High repetition.** Across 50 reels the same 7-step loop appears
   over and over — disarm, isolate, uncover, reframe, justify,
   consequence, identity close. Most teaching content has more
   variance; this didn't.
2. **The operator names his own moves.** "Yin-yang" (Reel 37),
   "Heavy is the Crown" (Reels 10, 36, 47), "Two kinds of people,"
   "Acting as if" — explicit naming made the abstraction step easier.
3. **Live failure analysis.** Several reels (18, 38, 40, 50) show
   *bad* objection handling with the operator critiquing each move.
   Those negatives anchored what *not* to do.
4. **Multiple variants of the same reframe.** Several archetypes
   (price, partner, think-about-it) had 3–5 different transcript
   examples, letting the patterns be triangulated rather than
   guessed from a single case.

---

## The 7-step loop — source

The loop wasn't stated explicitly anywhere in the corpus. It was
extracted by abstracting across multiple transcripts that all ran
the same sequence. Cleanest single-reel example: **Reel 26**
(handling "I want to speak to others first") — runs all 7 steps in
order, with the operator narrating his own structure as he goes.

Supporting evidence for each step:

- **Disarm** — *"Not a problem at all"* / *"I don't have a gun to
  your head"* appears in Reels 3, 9, 13, 25, 32, 34, 35, 36, 45.
- **Isolate** — *"Money/wife/time aside for a second"* in Reels 3,
  10, 13, 20, 25, 32, 36.
- **Uncover** — *"What's the real concern between you and I?"* in
  Reels 3, 13, 25, 32. *"What are you wanting to go over?"* in
  Reels 15, 45.
- **Reframe** — see archetype-by-archetype mapping below.
- **Justify** — *"Why?"* / *"Why do you think that is?"* in Reels 21,
  26, 28, 36, 47.
- **Consequence** — *"What happens for the next 2 days, 2 weeks,
  2 months, 2 years?"* in Reels 13, 33, 34, 35, 48.
- **Identity close** — *"What decision do you feel like you need to
  make to put yourself in the best possible position?"* in Reels 6,
  10, 13, 26, 32, 33, 35, 36, 47.

## Negative examples (what *not* to do)

Several reels feature the operator critiquing other reps' bad
objection handling. These are useful as anti-patterns:

- **Reel 18** — Rep tries to pitch a service, gets a no, then offers
  *"insight"* without isolating, uncovering, or reframing. Operator
  rates 4/10. Shows the cost of skipping the loop entirely.
- **Reel 25** — Rep has correct reframe content but delivers it with
  flat affect ("personality of a wet cardboard"). Shows the cost of
  ignoring tonality — a perfect reframe with no humanity doesn't land.
- **Reel 38** — Rep makes a cold call hunched over with shallow
  voice. Operator's note: *"if he just sat up, shoulders back, spoke
  from the diaphragm, he'd feel and sound more confident."* Shows the
  body-voice link.
- **Reel 40** — Rep pitches free work without uncovering pain first.
  Operator: *"something is only as valuable as the size of the problem.
  You have to uncover more pain before pitching."* Shows the cost of
  pitching before the pain is established.
- **Reel 46** — Rep stacks three reframes back-to-back with no pauses.
  Operator: *"You're not giving them time to process."* Shows the
  cost of pacing too fast.
- **Reel 48** — Rep agrees with the prospect's self-defeating framing
  ("you're right, you don't need that level of support") and discounts
  the offer instead of holding the frame. Operator's note: *"what does
  it signal? That what you originally pitched was unnecessary. You're
  scamming yourself."* Shows the cost of letting the prospect set the
  frame.
- **Reel 50** — Rep answers every question the prospect asks, ceding
  control of the conversation. Operator: *"redirect — every time they
  ask, you take control."* Shows the cost of being question-led
  rather than question-leading.

These negative cases helped calibrate the *Common mistakes* section
in the main SKILL.md.

## What was deliberately *not* carried over

Some content from the corpus was intentionally excluded from the
skill because it doesn't generalize or doesn't fit a professional
context:

- **Closer-bro vocabulary.** *"Fucking poor person,"* *"retard,"*
  references to physical attractiveness, "bro" energy throughout.
  This is the speaker's personal voice, not the framework.
- **High-ticket-only buyer assumptions.** Many transcripts assume the
  prospect is a 22-year-old male considering a $10K sales course. The
  reframes generalize, but the analogies (showing your tits online,
  fitness-focused examples) don't. The skill instructs the model to
  swap analogies to match the buyer.
- **Aggressive closes.** *"And the CVC?"* (Reel 34) — direct payment
  collection mid-emotional-moment. Works in high-ticket; hostile in
  enterprise B2B.
- **Theatrical / shock-value reels.** Reels 1, 14, 16, 17, 19, 20, 22,
  24, 27, 39, 41, 42, 49 are content reels (motivation, callouts,
  meta-commentary) rather than objection demos. Tonality and
  philosophy lifted; specific tactical content not used.

## Confidence calibration

For each archetype, my confidence that the reframe pattern works
*outside* the source operator's context:

| Archetype | Confidence | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Price | High | 4+ source examples, all converge on same structure. Generalizes well across registers. |
| Stall | High | Both end-of-call and mid-call variants documented. The "what are you wanting to go over?" probe is universal. |
| Authority | Medium-high | Heavy-is-the-Crown is sharp but works best B2C. For B2B with real co-decision-makers, propose a joint call instead of bypassing. |
| Comparison | High | Yin-Yang has 2 documented sources and an explicit framework explanation. |
| Time | Medium-high | Single core reframe, well-documented. |
| Skepticism | Medium | Only 1 source example. The pattern is sound but less battle-tested. |
| Self-doubt | Medium | Works on identity-attached prospects. Backfires in enterprise B2B without softening — flagged in the library. |
| Resistance | Medium | High-stakes reframe; only deploy if you genuinely haven't been pushy. |
| Avoidance | Medium-high | Doubles as a disqualification filter, which makes it lower-risk. |
| Universal fallback (Two Kinds of People) | High | Most documented pattern in the corpus. |

## Open questions / known limitations

1. **The corpus is one operator.** Pattern repetition gives confidence
   that *this operator* runs this loop consistently. It doesn't prove
   this loop is the optimal universal framework. Other operators (e.g.,
   Sandler, Challenger Sale, Straight Line) use overlapping but distinct
   structures.
2. **Selection bias toward closes.** The transcripts that survive into
   public content are mostly successful closes. The framework's
   true close rate is unknown.
3. **B2C bias.** The corpus is overwhelmingly direct-to-consumer (sales
   coaching, fitness, courses). The B2B / enterprise adaptations
   throughout the skill are extrapolations and should be tested against
   actual B2B calls before being treated as battle-tested.
4. **Cultural / regional fit.** The operator is North American. The
   tonality (especially "between you and I" and casual disarms) may
   land differently in other markets — particularly the more reserved
   business cultures of e.g. Northern Europe or Japan. For European
   markets, the user's manufacturing-sector context may want a more
   formal register; the skill instructs the model to adapt.
