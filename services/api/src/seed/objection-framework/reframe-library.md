# Reframe Library

For each objection archetype: when it shows up, what the surface vs real
concern usually is, the primary reframe pattern with language, and source
attribution. Read this file before generating any response — pattern
selection is what makes the difference between a sharp reply and a
generic one.

---

## How to read this file

Each entry has:

- **Surface trigger** — verbatim phrases that signal this archetype.
- **Real concern (usually)** — what the prospect is actually worried
  about. Use this to test your isolate step.
- **Primary reframe** — the named move and its template language.
- **Backup reframes** — alternates if the primary doesn't land.
- **Source** — which transcript the pattern was lifted from, for
  reference.

Templates use `{placeholders}` you fill in from the buyer context.

## 1. PRICE — "It's too expensive / out of budget / can't afford"

**Surface triggers:**
- "It's a lot of money."
- "I can't afford that."
- "$48K is too expensive."
- "It's outside our budget right now."
- "I need a payment plan."

**Real concern (usually):** Not the price itself — it's *risk* (will this
work for me?) or *commitment* (am I sure enough to lock this in?). The
isolate step exposes which.

### Primary reframe: Compared-to-What

The move: turn an absolute statement ("expensive") into a comparison the
prospect has to answer.

**Template:**
> "Compared to what, can I ask? Is it expensive compared to where you
> are now, or compared to where you want to be — at {desired_state}?
> Because for the version of you already at {desired_state}, do you feel
> like it would be expensive to him?"

**Why it works:** Forces them to compare investment against opportunity,
not against their current bank balance.

**Source:** Reels 6, 45 — *"Is it too expensive compared to like where
you are now, or kind of compared to where you want to be?"*

### Backup reframe: Opportunity Cost (Two Kinds of People)

**Template:**
> "Two kinds of people. There's the worker, who thinks about the price
> of everything — *this costs X, this costs Y*. And there's the
> {operator/owner/founder}, who thinks about opportunity cost. Like, look
> at any of these {software founder billionaires / category equivalents
> for your buyer} — when they were 5 years in, broke, sleeping on a
> couch, if all they thought about was price, would they have made it?
> No. So the question isn't whether {price} is expensive. It's whether
> you can afford *not* to fix this."

**Source:** Reel 35.

### Backup reframe: Acting As If

**Template:**
> "The hard thing about getting to {desired_state} is you kind of have
> to start acting like the person who's already there. If you had a
> {fat person / out-of-shape person / equivalent} who said *I'll start
> working out after I lose the weight*, what would you say? Exactly.
> Same thing here. The version of you at {desired_state} doesn't make
> decisions like the version of you at {current_state}. So what does
> that {desired-state} version do right now?"

**Source:** Reel 6.

**B2B adaptation:** Replace the fitness analogy with something
register-appropriate. *"If you had a CEO trying to scale who said I'll
hire senior people once we hit $100M ARR…"* Same structure, professional
register.

## 2. STALL — "I need to think about it / sleep on it"

**Surface triggers:**
- "Let me think about it."
- "I want to sleep on it."
- "Can I get back to you next week?"
- "I need to take some time."

**Real concern (usually):** A specific objection they haven't named yet —
often price, partner, or risk. Or, if delivered mid-call after a reframe,
it's resistance to the reframe itself.

### Primary reframe (end of call): What are you wanting to go over?

The move: convert vague "thinking" into a specific question you can
actually handle.

**Template:**
> "Not a problem at all. I guess — what are you actually wanting to go
> over in your head, like from now till then, just to see if I can help?"

That single question forces them to name the real concern. Then handle
that concern.

**Source:** Reel 15 — *"What are you wanting to go over in your head?
Like from now till then, just to see if I can help."*

### Primary reframe (mid-call, mid-reframe): Way of Thinking

If they hit you with "let me think about it" while you're still
reframing, it's not a stall — it's a way-of-thinking objection.

**Template:**
> "Yeah, that makes complete sense, man — we all make decisions based on
> our perspective, right? And would you agree the way we make decisions
> kind of shapes the results we get? So between you and I, on a scale of
> 1 to 10, where do you actually feel you are right now in terms of
> {their domain}? …Why not a 10?"

After they list problems: *"So that same way of thinking that got you
to a 3 — are you okay with letting it dictate the future?"*

**Source:** Reel 2.

### Backup: Fake Time

**Template:**
> "Yeah, no problem at all. How much time are you looking for —
> few days, a week? Okay, I have {X} or {Y} next week, which works
> better? …Cool. Now, just so I can use that time — what would you
> want to have answered by then?"

The booking lowers their guard. The follow-up question still surfaces
the real concern.

**Caveat from source (Reel 15):** This works on older buyers but feels
slow to younger / more impatient buyers. Default to "what are you
wanting to go over?" unless the buyer profile is older / more deliberate.

## 3. AUTHORITY — "I need to talk to my wife / partner / co-founder"

**Surface triggers:**
- "I need to run this by my wife/husband/partner."
- "I have to check with my co-founder."
- "I need to discuss with my board."

**Real concern (usually):** Either (a) genuine need to align with a real
decision-maker, or (b) the prospect is using the partner as cover for
their own indecision. The isolate step exposes which.

### Primary reframe: Heavy is the Crown

The move: shift the question from "should I get permission" to "whose
responsibility is this outcome?"

**Template (long form):**
> "{Partner} aside for a second — between you and I, do you actually feel
> like this gets you to {goal}? …Okay. Have you heard the saying *heavy
> is the head that wears the crown*? The idea is the weight of the crown
> isn't the gold or the jewels — it's the decisions you make when you're
> wearing it. Back in the day the king was the one sending an army this
> way, starting a war that way — responsible for hundreds of thousands
> of lives. Same way for you — who's actually going to be the one
> {executing the work / making the calls / running the team}? …You. So
> who has actual power to change this? …You. So whose responsibility do
> you really feel it is to make the decisions that get you to {goal}?
> …Mine. And what happens if you put that responsibility on anyone else
> — your {partner}, your {co-founder}, anyone who doesn't have your
> context? …I won't succeed. So what decision do you feel like you need
> to make as the {king/operator/owner} of this {household/company} to
> put yourself in the best possible position?"

**Source:** Reels 10, 36, 47.

### Primary reframe (short form, voice-agent friendly): Reduce-to-Ridiculous

For shorter calls or voice agents, skip the king metaphor and run
straight to the absurd version.

**Template:**
> "Of course. Quick one though — when this thing is up and running,
> is your {partner} going to be on the calls with you? Sitting there
> saying *honey, do this, say that*? …No. So who's actually responsible
> for making this work — you or them? …Me. So why are you putting that
> decision on them?"

**Source:** Reel 36.

### When to skip this reframe

If the prospect's "partner" is actually a co-CEO, board, or genuine
financial co-decider with veto power, don't try to bypass them — propose
a joint call instead. The Heavy-is-the-Crown move only works when the
prospect is the actual decision-maker hiding behind a softer "no."

## 4. COMPARISON — "I want to speak to other vendors first"

**Surface triggers:**
- "I want to talk to other companies."
- "Let me get a couple of quotes."
- "I want to do my due diligence."

**Real concern (usually):** Fear of being wrong / seeking certainty. They
want to outsource the decision to a comparison.

### Primary reframe: Yin-Yang (Perfection vs. Progress)

The 7-step move (most explicit example in Reel 26):

**Template:**

*Step 1 — surface the certainty trap:*
> "Completely understand. Quick question though — how are you actually
> going to know whether someone's going to get you a better result
> before you actually try?"
>
> *They say: "I guess I couldn't really know."*

*Step 2 — install the binary:*
> "Yeah. So in this world, two kinds of people. The first one is
> always looking for perfection — total certainty in everything. But the
> issue is, that doesn't exist. So they let perfection be the enemy of
> good and never actually take action. Then there's the second one. He
> knows perfection doesn't exist, so he just looks for what's good
> enough and takes steps in the right direction. Out of those two
> people — who actually goes further?"
>
> *They always pick #2.*

*Step 3 — make them justify:*
> "Why?"

*Step 4 — bring it back:*
> "Right. So can you see how that mentality of seeking absolute
> certainty is kind of what's been keeping us from actually getting
> training and getting to {goal}?"

*Step 5 — consequence:*
> "What happens if we let that same way of thinking dictate decisions
> for the next 2 days, 2 weeks, 2 months, 2 years?"

*Step 6 — mentality flip:*
> "So instead of seeking perfection, what mentality do you feel like
> you actually need?"

*Step 7 — close:*
> "Based on what we've covered — do you feel like this is good enough
> to take a step in the right direction toward {goal}?"

**Source:** Reels 26, 37 (the cleanest version of the framework).

### Backup: Biased-Testimonials Move

If the prospect deflects with *"I want to ask for testimonials"*:

**Template:**
> "Yeah, I can show you our best client testimonials — but think about
> it. Are those testimonials going to say *these guys are terrible,
> don't use them*? Or are they going to say something good? Right. So
> I don't want you making a decision based on a biased opinion. The
> real question is the same — how are you actually going to know before
> you try?"

**Source:** Reel 26.

## 5. TIME — "I don't have time"

**Surface triggers:**
- "I'm too busy right now."
- "I don't have bandwidth for this."
- "Maybe in 6 months."

**Real concern (usually):** Priority. Time isn't a quantity — it's an
allocation. The reframe surfaces that.

### Primary reframe: Time as Priority

**Template:**
> "Time's interesting because everyone has the same 24 hours. The
> question isn't really *do you have time* — it's *is this a priority?*
> Because look at any of these people who are jacked, running 9-figure
> companies, all of it — they don't have more hours in the day than
> you. They just allocate differently. So I guess the real question is
> — is this actually a priority for you? …Why? …And what happens if it
> stays a non-priority for the next 2 weeks, 2 months, 2 years?"

**Source:** Reels 13, 45.

## 6. SKEPTICISM — "I got burned before / I tried something like this"

**Surface triggers:**
- "I tried this with another vendor and it didn't work."
- "I've been burned before."
- "I'm skeptical of solutions like this."

**Real concern (usually):** Trust. They want a reason to believe this
won't be a repeat.

### Primary reframe: Doesn't Mean Nothing Works

**Template:**
> "That's completely fair — and I'd be skeptical too in your shoes.
> But just so I understand — does the fact that {previous vendor} didn't
> work mean nothing out there could actually solve {their problem}? …No,
> not necessarily. So what specifically would you need to see this time
> to feel confident it's different?"

**Source:** Reel 45.

The follow-up question is the real value — it converts skepticism into
a list of specific things you can demonstrate.

## 7. SELF-DOUBT — "I'm different / I have responsibilities / I can't"

**Surface triggers:**
- "I have a family, I can't take this risk."
- "I'm different from your other clients."
- "My situation is more complicated."
- "I just don't think I can do this."

**Real concern (usually):** Identity attachment. They've built a story
about why they can't, and the story is doing protective work.

### Primary reframe: Has It Occurred to You That Belief Is the Reason?

**Template:**
> "Look, I'm not going to push you. But — has it occurred to you that
> this belief — *I have too many responsibilities, I can't find a way* —
> might actually be the reason you haven't found one? Whatever you tell
> yourself, you hypnotize yourself into believing. So the question is —
> what's more important to you: holding onto that belief, or actually
> finding a way to {their goal} so you can {provide for family /
> achieve outcome they care about}?"

**Source:** Reel 33.

### Caveat

This reframe is high-risk on professional / enterprise calls. It works
on a 22-year-old considering a sales course. On a CTO at a €100M
manufacturer, it lands as condescending. For B2B enterprise, soften:

> "I hear you. Let me ask differently — if budget and risk weren't
> issues, would you say the technical fit is actually there? …Okay.
> So the question we're actually solving for is risk and procurement,
> not whether the platform fits. What would you need to see on the
> risk side to feel comfortable?"

Same underlying move (separate identity-attached objection from the
real solvable concern) — different register.

## 8. RESISTANCE — "You're being pushy"

**Surface triggers:**
- "I feel like you're being aggressive."
- "I'm not comfortable with this pressure."

**Real concern (usually):** Internal tension between *I want this* and
*I'm afraid of committing*. They're externalizing it onto the rep.

### Primary reframe: Flip to Internal Tension

**Template:**
> "Can I push back on that? How many times have I actually told you to
> buy on this call? …You haven't. All I've done is ask questions. So
> the tension you're feeling — has it occurred to you it might actually
> be internal? Part of you is ambitious, knows you should do this. Part
> of you is uncomfortable with the decision. Is that maybe what's
> coming up? …Yeah, I guess. Right. And growth is on the other side of
> comfort, isn't it? So why does growth happen outside the comfort
> zone? …Because you have to do hard things. So what do you think
> happens if we keep making decisions based on what's most comfortable
> for the next 2 days, 2 weeks, 2 months, 2 years?"

**Source:** Reel 34.

This one only works if you actually haven't been pushy. If you have
been, the reframe collapses — apologize, slow down, and re-earn trust.

## 9. AVOIDANCE — "Just send me the info"

**Surface triggers:**
- "Can you just email me a deck?"
- "Send me the pricing and I'll review."
- "I'll look it over and get back to you."

**Real concern (usually):** Low engagement / no real intent. They're
trying to end the call without saying no.

### Primary reframe: How Will You Know Before You Try?

**Template:**
> "Happy to send something over. Quick thing though — how are you
> actually going to know if this is a fit just from a deck? Half the
> point of this conversation is for you to ask the questions that don't
> show up in the materials. So — between now and a follow-up, what
> would you actually want answered for it to be worth booking time?"

If they can't name anything, they're not a buyer — disqualify gracefully
and move on. The reframe works as both a save and a filter.

## Universal fallback: Two Kinds of People

When the objection doesn't fit any archetype cleanly, fall back to the
universal binary:

**Template:**
> "Two kinds of people in this. The first one {has the limiting belief
> the prospect just expressed} → {bad outcome}. The second one
> {opposite belief} → {good outcome}. Out of those two — who actually
> goes further?"

Then run the standard Justify → Consequence → Close.

**Source:** Reel 37 (the operator's own description of why this works
on any limiting belief).

## Cross-cutting tonality reminders

- **Hot content, cold tone.** The hotter the content of the line, the
  flatter the delivery. *"I don't have a gun to your head"* delivered
  warmly defuses; delivered intensely escalates.
- **Pause after binaries.** *"Two kinds of people… [pause] there's the
  first one…"* The pause does the work.
- **"Between you and I"** is a status equalizer — use it before any
  hard question to lower defenses.
- **Never close on the offer.** Always close on the identity decision.
